**Cathedral vs Bazaar**

I believe the cathedral and bazaar models are extremely useful models, especially when we don't treat them as competing philosophies but as descriptions of where different kinds of work actually happen in software. The bazaar shows a more open and distributed route, where lots of contributors and contribution are being made. It's beneficial because with a lot of hands on deck, you discover things from issues to fix to new valuable features to add to edge cases. The cathedral details smaller and disciplined team that stay heavily focused on the intentional design of the architetcure. Since it's a tight knit circle of developers, conversations are easier to have, which allows for decisions to be made easier. Whether it's deciding what belongs, what doesn't, or what the your software fundamentally is. Based on my experience in Internships and some experieinces I've heard online, a lot of successful projects can run both models in different layers and ways.

AI coding tools are interesting because they don't map cleanly onto either model. AI Agents and tools are not contributors in the bazaar sense since they can't bring lived experience with the software, or opinions about what it should do. And they're not architects in the cathedral sense since they don't hold a design vision or make the kind of "this abstraction is wrong" calls that good systems design requires. While they can technically do these things, they require someone to prompt them to even get the process started and when they do it, their perspective, judgements, and opinions aren't coming from a place of creativty and unique life experience. What they do best is make software development cheaper as you hire less developers for the same task/project and they can translate developer intent into code very very well.

The practical effect is probably that AI amplifies whatever governance structure is already present. A team with strong architectural judgment can use it to execute faster without losing coherence. A project that already struggles with direction gets more coding volume and assistance but things won't neccesarily become clearer. The bazaar model is affected in an interesting way because now quantity might look different as the amount of developers can increase and code volume increase. To a point where projects get flooded with more contributions that look correct but don't fit the whole puzzle; the cathedral doesn't get largely affected as the small team setup is just adding on a code contirbutor that does exactly what they want to do.

**Team Progress**

My team has made good progress on the project so far. Not only were we all able to get a good grasp of the codebase and product but have been able to immerse ourselves in several issues so far. As a collective we've contributed to 4 issues in the form of verification, pinpointing the source of the issue, code changes and pull requests. We have not run in major issues, with slight issues coming in the form of lack of response from maintainers and difficulty finding new issues to tackle. To combat the latter issue, we've worked together on looking at issues and suggesting ones we believe would suit the person who needs it.

The other teams are facing similar issues, with many of them citing a complete lack of response from maintainers halting their progress. Some have found success in pinging the maintainers or joining town hall calls. Every team has reached the stage where they're making consistent contributions, with many teams having several members with pull requests open. Some teams had issues with the wiki page setup and that was something the professor called out for improvement. Other than that, it seems like everyone has a good grasp of their projects and how they can make an impact
