Most groups reported being in a very similar stage of progress, with the exception of two groups that seemed further along in their planning. The majority were still focused on finalizing their development environments and gaining a general understanding of the project structure, codebase, and community norms. A common theme across these groups was the challenge of navigating open-source contribution workflows, particularly around claiming issues and receiving timely responses from maintainers. This highlighted how contributing isn’t just about technical ability, but also about communication and timing within an active community. Some groups planned to get more involved by joining the project town hall meetings.

In contrast, two groups stood out for having more concrete direction. One group described working collaboratively on what sounded like a larger, more complex issue, suggesting they had already aligned on a shared goal and begun deeper technical engagement. The other group had clearly identified specific issues they intended to tackle, demonstrating a more defined short-term plan of action.

Our group fell somewhere in between these two extremes. We had successfully completed the setup of our development environment, which allowed us to begin exploring the repository more seriously. However, we were still in the process of understanding the codebase and getting a feel for the community dynamics, including how contributors interact and how issues are prioritized. While each of us had identified issues that interested us individually, we had not yet solidified a cohesive group plan or decided how to coordinate our efforts moving forward. This placed us in a transitional stage—past initial setup, but not yet fully aligned on execution.
